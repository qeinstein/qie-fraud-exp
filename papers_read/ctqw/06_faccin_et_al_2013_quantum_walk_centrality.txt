1. CORE MATHEMATICAL MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This foundational study in Physical Review X explores how quantum mechanics
alters the notion of node importance, degree distribution, and centrality in
complex networks.

In classical random walks, the average time a walker spends at a node (classical
centrality) is strictly proportional to its degree:
    p_k^{class} = d_k / (2 * |E|)
In a continuous-time quantum walk, the time-averaged occupation probability of a
node k, starting from an unbiased maximally mixed initial state
rho_0 = (1/N) * sum_v |v><v| = I / N, is given by:
    pi_k = lim_{T -> infty} (1/T) int_0^T Tr[|k><k| exp(-i H t) rho_0 exp(i H t)] dt
         = (1/N) * sum_n |<k|phi_n>|^4   (for non-degenerate spectrum)
where |phi_n> are the eigenvectors of the graph Hamiltonian H.

Key Findings:
1. Quantum-Classical Divergence:
   pi_k does NOT follow the classical degree d_k. Instead, due to multi-path
   constructive and destructive interference, low-degree nodes located at
   topological choke points or bottleneck paths can have massively amplified
   quantum centrality!
2. Quantum Trapping & Localization:
   Quantum walks can exhibit strong localization even in connected networks due
   to destructive interference across symmetric branches (Aharonov-Bohm-like
   cancellation), while certain core nodes experience resonant enhancement.


2. WHY CLASSICAL SYSTEMS FAIL ON THIS DYNAMIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hub Bias in Classical Graph Models:
   Classical centrality measures (PageRank, Degree, Eigenvector Centrality) are
   heavily biased toward raw transaction volume and high-degree accounts (e.g.,
   utility companies, telcos, supermarkets). Money mules, however, are deliberately
   low-degree accounts (1 inflow, 2-3 outflows) designed to fly under classical
   volume thresholds.
2. Blindness to Destructive Trapping:
   Classical models cannot model interference suppression. They assume that if an
   account has only 2 incoming transactions, it is insignificant. In contrast,
   quantum walk centrality identifies when a low-degree node acts as a resonant
   bridge in a multi-hop syndicate topology.


3. DIRECT VALUE & FORMULATION FOR OUR PROJECT (MONEY-MULE DETECTION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Faccin et al.'s formulation gives us a direct scalar feature: Quantum Walk
Centrality vs. Classical Degree Centrality Ratio!

Metric Definition:
For each account node k in the transaction subgraph:
1. Compute classical normalized degree centrality:
       C_{class}(k) = d_k / (2 * |E|)
2. Compute Faccin et al.'s Quantum Walk Centrality:
       C_{quant}(k) = (1/N) * sum_n |V_{k,n}|^4
   where V is the eigenvector matrix of H.
3. Compute the "Quantum Amplification Ratio":
       QAR(k) = C_{quant}(k) / (C_{class}(k) + 1e-6)

Detection Principle:
- Regular high-volume accounts (merchants): High classical degree, moderate QAR.
- Dormant/Low-profile money mules: Low classical degree, but highly resonant
  quantum centrality (high QAR) because they are topologically positioned to
  funnel coordinated flows.
This directly separates money mules from legitimate retail users!


4. CONCRETE CODE BLUEPRINT FOR qia_engine.py
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```python
import numpy as np

def compute_quantum_walk_centrality(H: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Computes Faccin et al. (PRX 2013) time-averaged quantum occupation centrality:
    pi_k = (1/N) * sum_n |V_{k,n}|^4
    Complexity: O(N^3) for eigendecomposition.
    """
    N = H.shape[0]
    w, V = np.linalg.eigh(H)
    # V is N x N eigenvector matrix
    # Compute sum over eigenvectors of the 4th power of absolute amplitudes
    pi = (1.0 / N) * np.sum(np.abs(V) ** 4, axis=1)
    return pi

def compute_quantum_amplification_ratio(H: np.ndarray, adj_matrix: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Computes ratio of quantum centrality to classical degree centrality.
    High ratio highlights topologically resonant low-degree nodes (mules).
    """
    pi_quant = compute_quantum_walk_centrality(H)
    degrees = np.sum(adj_matrix > 0, axis=1)
    total_deg = np.sum(degrees)
    if total_deg == 0:
        return np.ones_like(pi_quant)
    pi_class = degrees / total_deg
    
    # Quantum Amplification Ratio
    qar = pi_quant / (pi_class + 1e-6)
    return qar
```
