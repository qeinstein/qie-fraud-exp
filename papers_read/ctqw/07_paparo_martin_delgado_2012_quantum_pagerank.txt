1. CORE MATHEMATICAL MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This paper establishes the quantum counterpart to the classical Google PageRank
algorithm for directed complex networks.

Classical PageRank Dilemma:
Classical PageRank computes the principal eigenvector of the Google matrix:
    G = alpha * P + (1 - alpha) * (e * e^T) / N
where P is the row-stochastic transition matrix and alpha ~ 0.85 is the damping
factor introduced artificially to prevent the random walker from getting trapped
in "dangling nodes" (nodes with no out-links) or absorbing cycles.

Quantum Formulation:
The authors formulate a quantum walk on the directed bipartite directed network
Hilbert space H_N \otimes H_N (using a Szegedy/unitary walk quantization of
the directed transition matrix):
    U = S * (2 * Pi - I)
where Pi is the projector onto the directed link states |psi_j> = sum_k sqrt(P_{jk}) |j, k>,
and S is the swap operator S|j, k> = |k, j>.

The Quantum PageRank of node p is obtained via the instantaneous or time-averaged
occupation probability:
    I_q(p, t) = Tr[ (|p><p| \otimes I) U^t rho_0 (U^\dagger)^t ]
    <I_q(p)> = lim_{T -> infty} (1/T) sum_{t=0}^{T-1} I_q(p, t)

Key Theoretical Insights:
1. Resolving the Degeneracy Trap:
   In classical PageRank, nodes in symmetric branches or peripheral clusters
   often receive uniform or degenerate ranks. Quantum PageRank breaks this
   degeneracy due to quantum phase interference.
2. Sensitivity to Structural Fluctuations:
   Quantum PageRank is significantly more sensitive to network topology and
   directional asymmetries than classical PageRank. It highlights secondary
   hubs that classical PageRank dampens.


2. WHY CLASSICAL SYSTEMS FAIL ON THIS DYNAMIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Classical Damping Artifacts:
   In classical banking fraud engines, variants of PageRank (e.g., Personalized
   PageRank, PPR) are frequently used to trace stolen money. However, the classical
   damping factor (1 - alpha) causes the signal to decay exponentially with path
   length: at distance k from the victim, the influence is bounded by alpha^k.
   By hop 4, alpha^4 = 0.85^4 = 0.52; by hop 6, alpha^6 = 0.37. The signal fades
   into uniform damping noise.
2. Inability to Distinguish Layered Funnels from Dangling Leaves:
   Classical PageRank treats an account that does not immediately disperse funds
   as an ordinary dangling leaf, distributing its damping probability equally
   across all N nodes in the entire network.


3. DIRECT VALUE & FORMULATION FOR OUR PROJECT (MONEY-MULE DETECTION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Paparo & Martin-Delgado provide the mathematical justification for replacing
Personalized PageRank with Quantum Walk Ranking in transaction graphs:

1. Deep Chain Visibility:
   Because the unitary evolution preserves norm without artificial damping
   dissipation (alpha = 1), quantum walk probability travels undampened through
   4-hop, 5-hop, and 6-hop mule laundering chains.
2. Mule Funnel Ranking:
   Money mules that collect micro-transfers before cashing out exhibit higher
   relative Quantum PageRank variance compared to classical PPR:
       Quantum_Rank_Delta(v) = Rank_{quant}(v) - Rank_{PPR}(v)
   Nodes with high positive Delta are topological accumulators that evade
   classical PageRank due to low degree, but are surfaced by quantum interference.

```python
import numpy as np
import scipy.linalg

def compute_quantum_directed_rank(H_directed: np.ndarray, source_idx: int, t_steps: list) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Computes Quantum Walk Ranking vector from a source transaction node
    without artificial exponential damping decay.
    """
    N = H_directed.shape[0]
    w, V = np.linalg.eigh(H_directed)
    
    # Initial state localized at victim/source account
    psi_0 = np.zeros(N, dtype=np.complex128)
    psi_0[source_idx] = 1.0
    
    c = V.conj().T @ psi_0  # Projection coefficients
    
    time_avg_prob = np.zeros(N)
    for t in t_steps:
        # |psi(t)> = V @ diag(exp(-i * w * t)) @ c
        psi_t = V @ (np.exp(-1j * w * t) * c)
        prob_t = np.abs(psi_t) ** 2
        time_avg_prob += prob_t
        
    time_avg_prob /= len(t_steps)
    return time_avg_prob
```
