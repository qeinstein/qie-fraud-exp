1. CORE MATHEMATICAL MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This landmark review bridges classical complex network theory and quantum walks,
formalizing how quantum transport, quantum interference, and multiplex/multilayer
dynamics reveal topological features undetectable by classical network analysis.

Key Theoretical Concepts:
1. Quantum Transition Probability Density Matrix:
   The continuous-time quantum walk transition probability tensor between all
   node pairs is governed by:
       T_{ij}(t) = |<j| exp(-i * H * t) |i>|^2
   Unlike classical Markov transition matrices, T(t) is symmetric and bistochastic
   (sum_i T_{ij} = 1, sum_j T_{ij} = 1) even for unnormalized Laplacians,
   guaranteeing energy and information conservation.

2. Quantum Community Detection & Multiscale Partitioning:
   In classical community detection (e.g., Infomap, Louvain), random walkers
   become trapped in dense communities. In quantum networks, community boundaries
   act as coherent phase barriers. The time parameter t acts as a continuous
   "microscope resolution":
   - Small t (t -> 0): Quantum walker probes immediate 1-hop neighborhoods.
   - Intermediate t: Quantum amplitudes undergo constructive interference inside
     tight structural modules (mule rings).
   - Large t: Walker probes global network symmetries and conserved subspaces.

3. Von Neumann Entropy of Time-Evolved Network:
   S(t) = -Tr[rho(t) * log_2(rho(t))].
   The rate of entropy production dS/dt indicates whether the underlying graph is
   modular (slow entropy generation) or random/disordered (rapid entropy saturation).


2. WHY CLASSICAL SYSTEMS FAIL ON THIS DYNAMIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Over-Partitioning vs. Over-Smoothing:
   Classical modularity optimization (Louvain) frequently suffers from the
   "resolution limit": it fails to detect small, tight communities (like a 5-node
   mule ring) when embedded in a massive banking graph of millions of transactions.
2. Classical Diffusion Blur:
   Classical random walks diffuse into background noise, losing the phase
   signatures that distinguish malicious tightly-coupled subgraphs from benign
   commercial transaction clusters.

Biamonte et al. provides the theoretical justification for our Case Partitioning

1. Multiscale Quantum Resolution Filtering:

2. Quantum Modular Partitioning (Case Grouping):

import numpy as np

def compute_multiscale_quantum_affinity(H: np.ndarray, t_scales: list = [0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0]) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Computes Biamonte et al. multiscale quantum affinity matrix for case clustering:
    A_{quant}[i, j] = (1/K) sum_k |<j| exp(-i * H * t_k) |i>|^2
    """
    N = H.shape[0]
    w, V = np.linalg.eigh(H)
    A_quant = np.zeros((N, N), dtype=np.float64)
    
    for t in t_scales:
        U_t = V @ np.diag(np.exp(-1j * w * t)) @ V.conj().T
        T_t = np.abs(U_t) ** 2
        A_quant += T_t
        
    A_quant /= len(t_scales)
    return A_quant

def cluster_mule_ring_nodes(A_quant: np.ndarray, seed_node: int, threshold: float = 0.15) -> list:
    """
    Identifies all account nodes belonging to the same coherent mule ring
    originating from or linked to seed_node.
    """
    affinities = A_quant[seed_node, :]
    ring_members = [int(i) for i in np.where(affinities > threshold)[0]]
    return ring_members
